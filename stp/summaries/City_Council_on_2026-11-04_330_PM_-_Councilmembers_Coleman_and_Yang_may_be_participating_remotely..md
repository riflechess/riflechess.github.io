#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting  
**Purpose:** Regular City Council meeting to discuss and vote on ordinances, resolutions, and public hearings regarding city operations, zoning, and waste management.  
**Date/Time:** November 4, 2026, at 3:30 PM.  
**Context:** Several items were laid over for further hearings or final adoption in late November and early December.

#### Summary
**Commission Stipends and Compensation**
There was a discussion regarding an ordinance to increase stipends for members of city commissions and boards (such as the PCARC). The goal is to improve recruitment, retention, and diversity by making it easier for residents to volunteer despite childcare or work demands. 
*   **Point of Contention:** Councilmember Johnson expressed concern regarding the specific wording of the ordinance. She argued that the current "strike-through" language removes specific stipend amounts and replaces them with a "catch-all" allowing the City Director to set the amounts. She argued that for transparency, the ordinance should clearly state the expected amounts so volunteers know what to expect. Councilmember Coleman noted that while she is open to feedback on the mechanism, the bill has already been reviewed by several city departments. The item was laid over for a public hearing on November 18.

**Zoning and Solar Expansion**
The council reviewed a package of minor zoning code amendments. A significant portion of this update involves making solar panels more accessible for residents with narrow lots. Currently, technicalities regarding property setbacks can make solar projects financially unfeasible for some homeowners. The proposed change would allow panels to be placed closer to the roof edge, which is expected to encourage more community members to adopt solar energy.

**Mobile Saunas on Park Property**
A public hearing was held regarding the licensing of mobile saunas on city park property. Multiple members of the public testified in favor of the proposal, highlighting the health benefits of saunas and their role in building community connections. The council ultimately laid the matter over until November 18 for final adoption.

**Business Growth and Zoning (1201 Rice Street)**
The council discussed a rezoning request for 1201 Rice Street from a Traditional Neighborhood District to a General Business District. The applicant, Rice Holding Company (Mr. Tire), plans to provide car repairs, sales, and rentals. Public testimony supported the move, noting that it would create local jobs and provide essential transportation services to the neighborhood. The matter was laid over for a final vote on November 18.

**Waste Management and Utility Fees (2027 Budget)**
Several items were discussed regarding the 2027 budget for waste and utility services:
*   **Solid Waste:** A new fee is proposed for residential properties with five or more units to cover waste reduction and public space cleaning (such as "Big Belly" bins in parks).
*   **Recycling:** A modest increase in fees was proposed for both small and large residential units to account for contract increases and to fund recycling programs. The city is also seeking over $800,000 in state grant funds for 2027 to support these efforts.
*   **Water/Sewer:** Resolutions were laid over until December 2 to establish water, sanitary sewer, and storm sewer rates for 2027.

**Additional Items**
*   **Contract Awards:** The council approved a contract for enhanced street sweeping in the Capital Region and a resolution to budget funds for the 2027 Clean Energy Tax Credit.
*   **Development:** The city approved a project for Unreal LLC near 400 Snelling Avenue North to expand their facilities in the Snelling University complex.
