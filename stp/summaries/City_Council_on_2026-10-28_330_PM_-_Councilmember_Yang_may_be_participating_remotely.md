#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting
**Purpose:** Discussion and voting on various ordinances, resolutions, and zoning appeals.
**Date/Time:** October 28, 2026, at 3:30 PM
**Context:** Several members participated remotely. The meeting included several first readings, public hearings, and the adoption of resolutions regarding public safety and city services.

#### Summary
**Mobile Saunas Pilot Program (Item 14)**
The Council reviewed a proposal to launch a pilot program for mobile wood saunas in regional parks, starting at Como Regional Park. The proposal includes a new license category for private operators with a fee of $106. The program is intended to be a revenue generator for the city and will be open to the public for reservations. It will move to a public hearing next week.

**Garbage Service Rates (Item 16)**
The city presented the proposed 2027 garbage rates. Key highlights include:
*   A 3.5% increase in collection and bulky item fees.
*   A 12% increase in disposal fees (passing costs to the county).
*   Several quality-of-life updates, including enhanced safety for haulers, a new customer service position to handle more calls, and the expansion of programs like "cart sharing" and "food scrap" collection.

**Liquor Licenses at University/Snelling (Item 17)**
A public hearing was held regarding a new commercial development district to allow for three on-sale liquor licenses at the University and Snelling intersection. While the council expressed excitement for the area's growth, there was some discussion regarding the specific wording of the ordinance to ensure the pace of development remains manageable for the community. "Version 2" of the ordinance was adopted.

**Contractor Wages for Housing (Item 18)**
The council held a hearing on an ordinance allowing federal residential prevailing wage rates for carpenters on certain city-financed wood frame construction projects (specifically those under six stories). This is intended to facilitate the construction of low-income housing.

**Public Safety and Police Grants (Items 22 & 23)**
The Council unanimously approved two resolutions for the police department:
*   Accepting a **$22,000 donation** for the Women's Leadership Conference and the Honor Guard.
*   Accepting a **$508,350 "Toward Zero Deaths" grant** for traffic enforcement, focusing on impaired driving, pedestrian safety, and speeding.

**Tree of Lights Noise Variance (Item 20)**
There was significant discussion regarding the noise levels for the "Tree of Lights" at Rice Park. Neighbors living nearby expressed concerns about high decibel levels and heavy bass. The Salvation Army also expressed concern about maintaining the quality of the show. A compromise was reached and adopted in "Version 2," which lowers the decibel limit to 80 at certain times and mandates lower bass levels and the repositioning of speakers to protect nearby residents.

**Contested Zoning Appeal: 2094 Goodrich Avenue (Item 21)**
This was the most contentious item of the meeting. The council heard from numerous residents and a legal representative regarding a 18-bedroom "triplex" with a massive 2,500-square-foot common area.
*   **The Dispute:** Neighbors and some council members expressed concern that the building is not a standard triplex but is instead a "rooming house" or "partyplex" that would create significant safety, noise, and traffic issues for the neighborhood. They argued that the city’s current "triplex" label for the building is a loophole that bypasses necessary oversight.
*   **The Vote:** The motion to **deny the appeal** (which would uphold the current "triplex" classification and keep the building as is) was **not unanimous**. While five council members voted in favor of denying the appeal, **Councilmember Bowie voted against it**, indicating her concern over the risks of the current ruling and the potential for safety/oversight failures.

**Consolidated Items (24-31)**
A series of items regarding various city operations and minor updates were approved as part of a consent agenda.
