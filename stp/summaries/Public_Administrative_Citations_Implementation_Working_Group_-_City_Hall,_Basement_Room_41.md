#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Public Administrative Citations Implementation Working Group
**Purpose:** To discuss the development, implementation, and policy design of a new administrative citation ordinance.
**Context:** The meeting serves as a platform for public input on how the city will transition to using administrative citations—a "middle ground" enforcement tool—to ensure compliance with city codes. This follows a 2025 voter referendum that granted the city the authority to issue civil fines for code violations.

#### Summary
The meeting focused on the legislative progress and policy framework for a new system of administrative citations. The goal is to create a tool that sits between a simple warning (like a notice to mow a lawn) and a criminal citation, allowing the city to penalize non-compliance more effectively while avoiding the high legal threshold of criminal prosecution.

**Key Decisions and Policy Highlights:**
*   **Implementation Goals:** The system is designed to ensure consistent and timely enforcement of city codes. It is not intended to replace existing tools like license revocations or criminal citations but rather to provide a more efficient way to address moderate violations.
*   **Legal and Ethical Standards:** The city is working to ensure the ordinance meets constitutional requirements for "due process," including adequate notice, a meaningful opportunity to be heard, and an impartial hearing process.
*   **Equity and Appeals:** A significant portion of the discussion centered on the charter’s requirement that fines be imposed equitably. The process must allow for the consideration of an individual's financial circumstances (e.g., ability to pay) during the appeal process.
*   **Targeted Areas for Enforcement:** While the final list is still being refined, the city discussed several areas where these citations will likely be first implemented:
    *   **Public Safety and Quality of Life:** Animal control (e.g., dog bites), building code violations, and sidewalk/driveway permit issues.
    *   **Business and Housing:** Wage theft, minimum wage violations, tenant protections, and issues related to short-term rentals (e.g., Airbnb).
    *   **Property Maintenance:** Issues currently addressed with citations or "warnings" that lack an adequate enforcement mechanism for non-compliance.

**Points of Discussion and Contentious Areas:**
*   **The "Middle" Gap:** A significant point of discussion was the "missing middle" in current enforcement. Currently, if a property owner fails to correct a minor violation (like tall grass), the city often has no way to penalize them without pursuing a full criminal citation, which is a much more serious legal action.
*   **Complexity of the Draft:** The current draft of the ordinance is undergoing review by the City Attorney. The current version is quite complex (approximately 15 pages), and the working group's role is to help simplify these concepts into a workable public-facing policy.
*   **Upcoming Legislation:** The ordinance will eventually go through three readings. The city plans to introduce the core ordinance and the first 5–7 specific areas of enforcement simultaneously to provide a clear picture of what the program entails at the first public hearing.
