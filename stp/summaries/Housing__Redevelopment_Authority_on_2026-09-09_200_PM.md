#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Housing & Redevelopment Authority (HRA) Public Meeting
**Purpose:** Review of housing bond resolutions, downtown economic development funding, and urban revitalization projects.
**Date/Time:** September 9, 2026, at 2:00 PM
**Context:** This meeting addressed specific funding mechanisms for affordable housing and the "Downtown Vitality Fund" intended to bolster local businesses and public space.

#### Summary
The meeting focused on three primary areas: bond approvals for housing projects, economic development for the downtown core, and the conversion of a historic office building into affordable housing.

**Housing Bond Approvals**
*   The HRA unanimously approved (6-0) the resolution for conduit multifamily housing revenue bonds. This supports projects at 801 Mount Curve Boulevard, 2265 Hillcrest Avenue, and the Emma Norton Restoring Waters and Ellie Francis Court projects.

**Downtown Vitality Fund**
The board reviewed a plan to use $2 million specifically for economic development (of a total $5 million fund). The goal is to fill vacant storefronts and improve the "quality of life" in currently underserved downtown corridors.
*   **Funding Structure:** Projects under $100,000 are structured as grants; projects over $100,000 are structured as 4-year forgivable loans.
*   **Key Projects Highlighted:**
    *   **Endicott Arcade (134 5th Street East):** Approximately $799,800 for facade improvements and restoration.
    *   **428 Minnesota Street (With Love, Minnesota):** Approximately $682,200 to create a "civic destination" and retail space.
*   **Community Impact:** Commissioners and local business owners emphasized that these investments are aimed at removing the "bleakness" of certain blocks, specifically the stretch of 5th Street, to make the area feel safer and more welcoming for residents and pedestrians.

**HAM Building Conversion**
*   The HRA reviewed a proposal for the HAM building at 408 St. Peter Street.
*   **Project Scope:** The project involves converting a historic office building into 128 affordable housing units (at 60% AMI).
*   **Funding Request:** A request for up to $14 million in multifamily conduit revenue bonds was introduced. This will be up for a formal public hearing and final approval on September 23.

**Notable Developments & Safety**
*   Several local businesses—including the St. Paul Farmer's Market, a local café/bar, and Metronome Brewery—shared how these funds will help them maintain their presence in the city.
*   There was significant emphasis on "activation" of public spaces to improve the safety and aesthetic of the downtown area, particularly in areas that have remained vacant for long periods.
