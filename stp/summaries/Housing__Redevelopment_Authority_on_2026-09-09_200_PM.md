#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Housing & Redevelopment Authority (HRA) Meeting
**Purpose:** Review of housing project bonds, the Downtown Vitality Fund, and upcoming economic development recommendations.
**Date/Time:** September 9, 2026, at 2:00 PM
**Context:** City of Saint Paul; the meeting focused on multi-family housing, downtown economic revitalization, and a specific large-scale office-to-housing conversion.

#### Summary
**Housing Revenue Bonds (RESPH 26-254)**
The HRA approved a resolution to authorize the reissuance of conduit multifamily housing revenue bonds. This funding is slated for several projects, including 801 Mount Curve Boulevard, 2265 Hillcrest Avenue, Emma Norton Restoring Waters, and Ellie Francis Court. The vote was unanimous (6-0).

**Downtown Vitality Fund (SR 26-190)**
A report was presented regarding the $2 million allocation for economic development within the Downtown Vitality Fund. The fund aims to revitalize downtown by filling vacant storefronts and supporting local businesses. 
*   **Grant/Loan Structure:** Projects under $100,000 are structured as grants. Projects over $100,000 are structured as forgivable loans (over a four-year period) to ensure long-term commitment to the downtown area.
*   **Major Projects:** Two significant projects requiring specific HRA approval at the next meeting (September 23rd) were highlighted:
    *   **Endicott Arcade (134 5th Street East):** A request for approximately $799,800 for facade improvements and building repairs to bring the vacant property back to market.
    *   **428 Minnesota Street:** A request for approximately $682,200 for the renovation of 11,000 square feet of retail space to be used as a civic destination.
*   **Business Impact:** Several local entities (St. Paul Farmer's Market, Metronome Brewery, and a local restaurant) expressed gratitude for the investment, noting how even smaller amounts can significantly improve safety and vibrancy in the downtown area.

**HAM Building Conversion (SR 26-191)**
Staff presented a proposal for the HAM building at 408 St. Peter Street. 
*   **Project Scope:** The project involves converting a historic office building into 128 housing units affordable at 60% of the Area Median Income (AMI).
*   **Funding:** The project will seek up to $14 million in multifamily conduit revenue bonds. 
*   **Status:** This item will move forward for a public hearing and a formal vote on September 23rd.
