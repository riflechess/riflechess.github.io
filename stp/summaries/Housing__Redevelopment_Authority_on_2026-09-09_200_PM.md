#### Meeting Info
**Meeting Type:** Housing & Redevelopment Authority (HRA) Meeting  
**Purpose:** Review of downtown vitality funding, bond issuances for housing, and urban renewal projects.  
**Date/Time:** September 9, 2026, at 2:00 PM  
**Context:** The meeting focused on significant investment in downtown St. Paul, including the Downtown Vitality Fund and the conversion of historic buildings into affordable housing.

#### Summary
The meeting covered three primary items involving downtown revitalization and housing infrastructure:

**1. Housing Bond Approval (Item 26-254)**
The HRA approved a resolution to authorize and re-issue conduit multifamily housing revenue bonds for projects at 801 Mount Curve Boulevard and 2265 Hillcrest Avenue (Emma Norton Restoring Waters and Ellie Francis Court). This was approved unanimously (6-0).

**2. Downtown Vitality Fund (Item 26-190)**
The committee reviewed recommendations for the Downtown Vitality Fund, which allocates $2 million for economic development and $3 million for housing. 
*   **Small Business Support:** Ten projects under $100,000 were approved administratively as grants to support various downtown businesses, including the St. Paul Farmer's Market and a local Ethiopian restaurant.
*   **Major Projects (For-given Loans):** Two large projects were highlighted that will move to a formal vote on September 23:
    *   **Endicott Arcade (134 S. 5th St. E):** A requested $799,800 for facade improvements and structural repairs to turn a long-vacant building into a retail space.
    *   **428 Minnesota Street:** A requested $682,200 to convert over 11,000 square feet of vacant space into a civic destination.
*   **Funding Structure:** Projects over $100,000 are structured as four-year forgivable loans to ensure the buildings remain active and occupied. 
*   **Community Impact:** Local business owners and representatives from "With Love, Minnesota" spoke in favor of these projects, noting that the current vacant stretches of the city can feel "bleak" at night; these investments are intended to improve public safety and quality of life by increasing foot traffic and activity.

**3. HAM Building Conversion (Item 26-191)**
The HRA received a report on the conversion of the historic HAM Building (408 St. Peter Street) from an office space into 128 affordable housing units. 
*   **Funding:** The project is seeking up to $14 million in multifamily conduit revenue bonds. 
*   **Details:** The conversion aims to keep the building's historic character while providing housing at 60% of the Area Median Income. A final public hearing and City Council vote for these bonds are scheduled for September 23.

**Upcoming Dates:**
The next HRA meeting is scheduled for **September 23**, where the final votes for the Downtown Vitality Fund's larger projects and the HAM building bonds will take place.
