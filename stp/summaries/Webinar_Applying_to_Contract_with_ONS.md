#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Webinar  
**Purpose:** To provide information and guidance for organizations applying for 2027 professional service contracts with the Office of Neighborhood Safety (ONS).  
**Context:** The meeting detailed the requirements, goals, and selection process for contracts aimed at improving public safety, mental health, and services for the unhoused in Saint Paul.

#### Summary
The primary purpose of this meeting was to outline the requirements for organizations seeking to partner with the Office of Neighborhood Safety for the 2027 calendar year. The Office of Neighborhood Safety focuses on crime prevention, violence reduction, and improving the quality of life through three main initiatives: Project Peace, Familiar Faces (homeless services), and the Neighborhood Safety Community Council (NSCC).

**Contract Terms and Budget**
*   **Contract Type:** These are professional service agreements, not grants. The city is "hiring" organizations to perform specific tasks.
*   **Budget Limits:** Each contract is capped at **$40,000** for the 2027 year. There is a minimum contract amount of **$10,000** for logistical purposes.
*   **Payment Structure:** Payments are made via a reimbursement model (services are provided first, and the organization invoices the city).
*   **Eligibility:** Applications are reviewed based on the organization's ability to serve Saint Paul residents within city limits.

**Key Priority Areas (The Five Needs)**
Contractors are encouraged to apply based on these specific community goals:
1.  **Culturally Specific Violence Prevention:** Targeting youth and young people in diverse communities (Mong-Khmer, Somali, Latino, and African-American).
2.  **Stabilization for Unhoused Neighbors:** Services for those frequently using emergency services or shelters, and those involved in the justice system.
3.  **Chemical and Mental Health Outreach:** Expanding services for individuals aged 25 and older, specifically aimed at interrupting drug use in public spaces like parks and libraries.
4.  **Capacity Building:** Targeted at small organizations (less than 10 years of operation) to improve their internal processes and service delivery.
5.  **Justice Involvement:** Providing workforce development and education for people at risk of entering the justice system.

**Selection and Oversight**
The selection process is overseen by the Neighborhood Safety Community Council (NSCC). A task force, composed mostly of community members, reviews and scores the applications. This highlights the community-driven nature of the selection process.

**Key Dates for Applicants**
*   **August 28:** Deadline to submit questions to the city (to be published in a FAQ on August 31).
*   **September 7:** Deadline to submit the final application and budget.
*   **September 25, 2026:** Date by which organizations will be notified of acceptance or denial.
*   **January 1, 2027:** Start date of the contract cycle.

**Points of Clarification/Contention**
While the meeting was informative and not contentious, a few specific points were clarified during the Q&A session:
*   **Budget Concerns:** A participant raised concerns about whether the city's current budget deficits would impact these contracts. The representative confirmed that these funds are secured for 2027 and that the office has already made adjustments to ensure these community contracts remain a priority.
*   **Contract Scale:** It was clarified that while the cap is $40,000, organizations do not need to "inflate" their projects to reach that amount; they should request what is necessary for their specific scope.
*   **Scope of Services:** It was clarified that while general human services (like food access) are important, the priority for these specific contracts is on violence prevention and the specific "needs" listed above.
