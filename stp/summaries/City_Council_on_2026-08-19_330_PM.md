#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting  
**Purpose:** Discussion of city ordinances, budget amendments, property management assessments, and public safety contracts.  
**Date/Time:** August 19, 2026, at 3:30 PM.

#### Summary

**Fire Department Leadership & Selection Process (Item 22)**  
The Council reviewed a proposal to establish a qualifications and examining committee for the next Fire Chief. While there was no public testimony on this specific item, several council members expressed strong support for the diverse "slate" of candidates currently being prepared. A representative from St. Paul Firefighters Local 21 attended and spoke in favor of the ordinance, emphasizing that the current selection process successfully balances community perspectives with professional expertise in fire and emergency medical services.

**Public Safety & Drone Technology (Items 25 & 26)**  
These items generated significant public pushback and sparked heated debate regarding city spending and technology usage:
*   **Item 25:** A resolution to adjust the capital improvement budget following bond issuances. During the hearing, several residents voiced strong opposition to the "expansion of a drone program" (specifically involving Skydio drones). Residents expressed concern that significant funds are being spent on high-tech equipment and "weapons of war" while other public services, such as libraries, face budget cuts and closures.
*   **Item 26:** A resolution regarding police department sub-grants for domestic abuse intervention. Public testimony was highly critical of the inclusion of AI-powered drones in city contracts. Testifiers expressed concerns over the company’s international reputation and questioned if these technologies improve community safety or simply increase surveillance.

**Local Infrastructure & Events (Items 23, 27, 28, 29)**  
*   **Sewage Construction:** The council approved a special sanitary sewer connection charge for the Como area, ensuring costs are attributed to new developments rather than existing residents.
*   **Sound Variances:** The city granted sound level variances for two upcoming community events: the "Walk for Recovery" and the "Nativity County Fair."
*   **Front Avenue Roadwork:** The public hearing regarding the mill and overlay program for Front Avenue was postponed until October 7th to allow more time for consideration.

**Property Management & Nuisance Cases (Items 30, 33, 36, 39, 44)**  
The council addressed several specific property issues involving boarded windows, vehicle removals, and utility shut-offs:
*   **Boarding Fees:** The Council reviewed two instances where the city was billed for emergency boarding of properties. While some management companies complained that they were not contacted before police took action, the Council moved to approve the fees as standard procedure was followed in both cases.
*   **947 Fremont Avenue:** Regarding a nuisance building with no progress on rehabilitation, the council opted for a two-week layover to see if any physical repairs could be confirmed before taking further enforcement action.
*   **314 Wyoming Street East:** A one-week extension was granted to an owner struggling to clear vehicles from a property due to delays in obtaining titles from overseas.
*   **1103 Chatsworth Street North:** The council approved a modified recommendation extending the deadline for utility restoration to October 15th, providing more time for a homeowner facing significant hardship with her electricity bill.

**Administrative Items (Item 35)**  
The matter regarding the removal of structures at 914 Central Avenue was referred back to the legislative hearing team until September 15th.
