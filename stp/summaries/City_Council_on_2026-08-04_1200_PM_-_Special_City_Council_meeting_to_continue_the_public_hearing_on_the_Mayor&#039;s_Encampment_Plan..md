#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Special City Council Meeting (Public Hearing)
**Purpose:** Continuation of public hearing regarding the Mayor’s Encampment Plan.
**Date/Time:** August 4, 2026, at 12:00 PM.
**Context:** This meeting served as a platform for residents and organizations to testify regarding the impending "sweep" (eviction) of the Fish Hatchery (Pig's Eye) Encampment, which was scheduled for the day following this hearing.

#### Summary
The session was dominated by public testimony from advocates, faith-based leaders, and people with lived experience regarding homelessness in St. Paul. While no final policy vote was taken during this specific hearing, there was significant tension regarding the timing of the enforcement action.

**Key Points of Contention:**
*   **Urgent Request for Delay:** A major point of contention was the "immediacy" of the planned eviction. Multiple speakers (including representatives from faith communities and local advocates) urged the Council to pause the sweep for 30 to 60 days. They argued that current county contracts are just beginning and that a delay is necessary to ensure residents aren't left without resources, safety, or a destination after being moved by police.
*   **Safety and Quality of Life:** Advocates warned that an immediate "sweep" would lead to significant harm for the unhoused population, specifically regarding potential violence during the eviction process, loss of personal property (including items required for health and identity), and increased risks of overdose without a coordinated transition plan. 

**Budget and Policy Proposals:**
*   **Allocation Critique:** Some speakers criticized the high cost of existing shelter programs ($20,000 per bed) and suggested that those funds, or similar figures, could be more effectively used to provide housing vouchers or "keys" to smaller units, which they argued would be more cost-effective than maintaining status quo services.
*   **Cost of Enforcement:** One speaker highlighted a discrepancy in spending, noting that it costs approximately $1,300–$2,000 in police resources per person to remove them from a site—money they suggested could instead fund basic necessities like new clothing, tools for employment, and identification documents.
*   **Alternative Solutions:** A "Tiny House Village" model was proposed as a viable alternative to the current system of traditional shelters and open-air encampments.

**Community Concerns:**
*   **Lack of Communication:** There was significant frustration regarding the perceived absence or lack of engagement from the Mayor’s office, with several speakers claiming their inquiries went unanswered while they sought an emergency moratorium on the eviction.
*   **Humanity and Advocacy:** Several residents emphasized that the individuals in these encampments are often seniors or people with complex medical needs who are currently "trapped" by a lack of available housing options.

The meeting concluded with a unanimous 5-0 vote to close the public hearing and adjourn.
