#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Special City Council Meeting / Public Hearing  
**Purpose:** Continuation of the public hearing regarding the Mayor’s Encampment Plan, specifically concerning the upcoming eviction of the Fish Hatchery encampment.  
**Date/Time:** August 4, 2026 | 12:00 PM – 1:00 PM  
**Context:** The meeting served as a platform for community members, advocacy groups, and faith organizations to provide testimony regarding the methods and timing of local encampment clearances.

#### Summary
The primary focus of this hearing was the public’s intense opposition to the immediate eviction of residents at the Fish Hatchery encampment. The session was dominated by public testimony rather than council deliberation, as it functioned as a dedicated window for community feedback on the Mayor's Encampment Plan.

**Key Concerns and Advocacy Points:**
*   **Safety and Quality of Life:** A major point of contention was the immediate danger to unhoused residents during a rapid "sweep." Advocates warned that without a transition plan, people would be "scattered," leading to an increase in overdoses and higher risks of police-related injuries. 
*   **Budgetary Criticism:** Several speakers critiqued the current allocation of funds, specifically questioning the high cost per bed ($20,000) and arguing that a significant portion of the $1 million investment would be better spent on rental assistance or "Tiny House Village" models, which were presented as more sustainable long-term solutions.
*   **Cost Analysis:** A speaker provided a data-driven comparison, noting that it costs approximately $1,375 to $2,000 to remove a single person from an encampment via police action—money they argued could instead provide months of housing, clothing, and essential documentation for those individuals.
*   **Call for Moratorium:** There was a widespread demand from the community (including faith-based organizations) for a **30-to-60-day moratorium** on evictions. This pause would allow time to coordinate with county partners and ensure that residents are moved into safe, managed locations rather than simply being displaced.

**Points of Contention:**
*   **Lack of Communication:** There was significant frustration expressed regarding the Mayor's absence from public meetings and what was described as a lack of responsiveness to community inquiries.
*   **Systemic Failures:** Testimony highlighted that while the city is moving forward with evictions, local advocates claim there are currently no adequate shelter beds available to accommodate those being removed "tomorrow."
*   **Differing Approaches:** While some speakers focused on the humanitarian and spiritual side of the issue, others provided specific policy alternatives, such as the Tiny House Village model, to move away from what they called a "policy of fear."

**Closing Actions:**
Following the conclusion of public testimony, the council moved to close the hearing. The motions to both close the public hearing and adjourn the meeting were passed unanimously (5-0).
