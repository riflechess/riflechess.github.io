#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Budget Committee Meeting
**Purpose:** Presentation and discussion of the 2027 Parks and Recreation Department budget.
**Date/Time:** September 2, 2026, at 10:00 AM
**Context:** The meeting focused on navigating budget pressures while maintaining essential city services, with a heavy emphasis on facility conditions, safety, and youth programming.

#### Summary
**Budget Overview and Service Adjustments**
The Parks and Recreation Department is facing significant pressure, as approximately 90% of their budget is tied to staffing and building costs. To avoid widespread layoffs or total facility shutdowns, the department proposed several "strategic reductions":
*   **Holiday Closures:** Non-hub recreation centers will close on additional holidays (MLK Junior Day, President’s Day, Juneteenth, and Christmas Eve) because of lower usage and higher staffing costs for holiday pay.
*   **Modified Hours:** Most "hub" sites (larger community centers) will shift their opening time from 9:00 AM to 10:00 AM.
*   **Youth Programs:** In a positive move for the community, programs like **RightTrack** and **Sprockets** are being transitioned to the general fund to ensure long-term sustainability for youth employment and out-of-school programming.
*   **Safety and Technology:** A portion of the budget is dedicated to "safety technology," including cameras and remote-access locking systems, to address rising safety incidents and "teen takeover" events at various locations.

**Facility Status and Infrastructure**
A significant portion of the meeting focused on the deteriorating state of specific buildings:
*   **Duluth and Case:** This facility is proposed for closure due to severe deterioration (falling siding, failing roof, and issues with HVAC/plumbing). While it is being replaced by a new community center, the current building is no longer safe or viable to maintain as-is.
*   **Highwood Hills:** The proposal involves reducing indoor operations at this site due to high maintenance costs and the poor condition of the interior space.

**Points of Contention and Disagreement**
There was significant tension regarding the impact of these cuts on the **East Side** of the city. 
*   **Equity Concerns:** Council members expressed deep frustration that the proposed budget disproportionately impacts the East Side. Specifically, they argued that the "reduction" of indoor services at Highwood Hills is effectively a full closure of a vital asset in a high-density area with limited alternatives.
*   **Value vs. Cost:** A major point of contention was the math behind the cuts. It was noted that the total savings from these various service reductions are only **$188,000**. Several council members argued that this relatively small amount of money does not justify the significant loss of public access and quality of life for residents in those specific neighborhoods.
*   **Transparency on Timelines:** There were concerns regarding the lack of a clear, firm timeline for the completion of the new Duluth and Case building, leading to fears that the community will be left without services for an extended period.
