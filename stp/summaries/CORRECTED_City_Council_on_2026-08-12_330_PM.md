#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting
**Purpose:** Regular meeting to review ordinances, public hearings, and city infrastructure updates.

#### Summary
**Youth Minimum Wage Reform (Item 18)**
The Council voted unanimously to adopt Ordinance 26-31, which removes the current policy that allows a lower minimum wage for workers between the ages of 14 and 17. The change aims to eliminate discrimination based on age, ensuring young people are paid the full city minimum wage when performing the same tasks as adult employees.

**Infrastructure & Community Assets (Items 20–23)**
Several routine resolutions were approved regarding local infrastructure:
*   **Sewerage:** Ratification of assessments for sewer replacement related to the Metro Transit gold line project.
*   **Property Development:** Approval of an alley right-of-way vacation at 779 Lexington Parkway South to facilitate a housing development.
*   **Waste Management:** The city accepted a donation of "Big Belly" brand trash and recycling stations from the Highland District Council.

**Public Events & Sound Variances (Items 24–27)**
The council approved several sound level variances for upcoming community events:
*   St. Paul Get Down (August 29th) at Raspberry Island Regional Park.
*   University of St. Thomas back-to-school pep rally and a football game against Winona State.
*   Selby Avenue Jazz Fest (September 12th).

**Fence Dispute & Compliance Extension (Item 29)**
This item involved significant discussion regarding a damaged fence at **369 Dale Street North**. There was considerable tension between the property owner and city staff:
*   **The Conflict:** The owner, Mr. Rosenblum, argued that his fence is structurally sound despite being hit by vehicles frequently (a recurring issue over 10 years). He requested it be accepted as-is or with minor adjustments. However, the Department of Safety and Inspections maintained that because of the damage from collisions, the structure was not in a professional state and required serious assessment.
*   **The Resolution:** To address the impasse, the council moved toward an "alternative means" of compliance. 
*   **Non-Unanimous Vote:** The final motion to approve the modified resolution—which grants an extension until October 26th for the owner to provide a professional engineer's statement and potential design modifications—passed **6 to 1**.
