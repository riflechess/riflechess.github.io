#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting
**Purpose:** General city business, including ordinances, resolutions, and public hearings.
**Date/Time:** August 12, 2026, at 3:30 PM.

#### Summary
The council addressed several items regarding labor laws, infrastructure, public events, and property maintenance.

**Key Decisions & Ordinances:**
*   **Youth Minimum Wage (Ordinance 26-31):** The council voted unanimously to eliminate the city's provisional 90-day minimum wage rate for workers aged 14 to 17. This change is intended to end discriminatory pay practices and support young people who provide essential income for their families during the current affordability crisis.
*   **Fire Chief Selection:** Ordinance 26-38 was adopted to establish a Qualifications and Examining Committee for the position of Fire Chief.
*   **Infrastructure & Donations:**
    *   Ratified sewer replacement assessments related to the Metro Transit 10-mile gold line project.
    *   Accepted a donation of a "Big Belly" trash and recycling station from the Highland District Council.
    *   Adjusted the operating budget to reflect judgment bond issuances.
*   **Public Events (Sound Variances):** Several sound variance requests were approved for upcoming community events, including:
    *   The St. Paul Get Down at Raspberry Island Park (Aug 29).
    *   A University of St. Thomas back-to-school pep rally (Sept 3).
    *   Selby Avenue Jazz Fest (Sept 12).
    *   A University of St. Thomas vs. Winona State football game (Aug 27).
*   **Property Development:** A petition was approved to vacate an alley right-of-way at 779 Lexington Parkway South to increase property density and support affordable housing.

**Contested Items & Public Safety:**
*   **Fence Abatement Appeal (369 Dale Street North):** This item involved a dispute over a damaged fence on the old Tiger Jacks lot. The owner argued that the structure was safe but expressed frustration over high maintenance costs due to frequent vehicle impacts at the intersection. 
    *   **The Conflict:** There was disagreement regarding whether the current fence met safety standards or if it needed total replacement. While the official recommendation suggested a deadline in September, the council eventually moved the deadline to **October 26th**. This extension allows time for an engineer's letter and a formal inspection to determine if the structure is "structurally sound."
    *   **The Vote:** This was not a unanimous vote (**6 in favor, 1 opposed**) due to discussions regarding the specific criteria for what constitutes a safe, professionally maintained structure.
