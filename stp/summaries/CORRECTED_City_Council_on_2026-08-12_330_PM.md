#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting  
**Purpose:** Routine business including ordinance updates, public hearings on property matters, and noise variances for local events.  
**Date/Time:** August 12, 2026, at 3:30 PM.

#### Summary
Several items were addressed during this meeting regarding city policy, infrastructure, and community life:

*   **Youth Wage Equality (Ordinance 26-31):** The council voted to eliminate the distinction in minimum wage for workers between the ages of 14 and 17. The goal is to ensure that younger workers are paid the same as their older counterparts for performing the same jobs, removing what was viewed as a discriminatory policy.
*   **Property & Infrastructure:** 
    *   The council ratified sewer replacement assessments related to the Metro Transit 10-mile gold line project.
    *   A "Big Belly" trash and recycling station donation from the Highland District Council was accepted for public use.
    *   An alley vacation at 779 Lexington Parkway South was approved to assist in increasing housing density and supporting affordable housing initiatives.
*   **Public Safety & Community Events:** The council approved multiple noise variance applications for upcoming local events, including the "St. Paul Get Down" (Aug 29), various University of St. Thomas events (Sept 3 and Aug 27), and the Selby Avenue Jazz Fest (Sept 12).
*   **Contested Item - Dale Street Fence Dispute:** There was significant discussion regarding a damaged fence at 369 Dale Street North. The owner of the property argued that despite its appearance, the fence is structurally sound and they have already invested significantly in repairs. The city’s enforcement department maintained that without an official engineer's certification confirming it meets code, the structure cannot be deemed "sound."
    *   **The Conflict:** The owner expressed frustration with the lack of clarity from inspectors and requested a final decision so the matter could go to court if needed. 
    *   **Resolution:** To ensure there is time for a professional engineer to provide a written certification and for the city to review those plans, the council opted to grant an extension. The deadline for compliance was moved to October 26th.
