#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting
**Purpose:** Legislative hearing, public hearings, and ordinance adoptions
**Date/Time:** August 12, 2026, at 3:30 PM
**Location:** St. Paul City Hall

#### Summary
The council addressed several items regarding labor standards, infrastructure, community events, and property maintenance.

**Key Decisions & Community Impact:**
*   **Youth Minimum Wage:** The Council approved Ordinance 26-31 to eliminate the city's provisional lower minimum wage rate for workers aged 14 to 17. This change aims to ensure pay equity between youth and adult workers. Council members highlighted that this move supports young people contributing to their families' incomes with dignity and equal pay for equal work.
*   **Public Safety & Infrastructure:**
    *   The council approved several sound level variances for upcoming community events, including the "St. Paul Get Down," a university pep rally, a jazz festival, and a football game.
    *   A resolution was passed to accept a donation of "Big Belly" garbage and recycling collection stations from the Highland District Council to support city operations.
    *   The council approved an alley vacation at 779 Lexington Parkway South to increase property density and promote affordable housing.
*   **Budget & Administrative:** Several resolutions were passed regarding sewer assessment ratifications, budget adjustments for judgment bonds, and the establishment of a committee for the Fire Chief position.

**Areas of Contention:**
*   **Fence Repair at 369 Dale Street North (Item 29):** This item involved an appeal by property owner Lucky Tiger Jack Rosenblum regarding a damaged fence that had been hit by a vehicle. 
    *   **The Conflict:** The owner testified that he is on a fixed income and believes the fence—while not perfect—has been reinforced enough to be "structurally sound." He requested the matter be settled immediately so it could move to court if necessary. Conversely, city staff recommended an extension of time to allow for a formal engineering review and inspection to ensure the structure meets safety codes. 
    *   **The Vote:** The council eventually agreed to grant an extension until October 26th for the owner to provide an engineer's letter or complete repairs. **The vote on this resolution was not unanimous; while it passed, there was a split (6 in favor, 1 opposed) regarding the specific language and timeline of the amendment.**
