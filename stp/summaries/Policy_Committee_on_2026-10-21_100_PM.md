#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Policy Committee Meeting  
**Purpose:** Discussion of standard operating procedures (SOPs) for honorary street co-naming.  
**Date/Time:** October 21, 2026, at 1:00 PM  
**Context:** The city previously ended the practice of street co-naming. This meeting served as a workshop to establish the formal criteria, process, and administrative oversight required to potentially resume the practice for future requests.

#### Summary
The committee discussed the framework for reinstating street co-naming, focusing on how to balance community desires for recognition with practical logistics and public safety.

**Key Policy & Logistics:**
*   **SOP Development:** There are currently two proposed versions of the SOPs (from Ward 1 and Ward 7). While they share many similarities, they differ slightly on which body would oversee the review process.
*   **Public Works Guidelines:** Public Works provided specific technical recommendations to ensure clarity:
    *   Co-names should only be used on continuous street segments (not interrupted by parks or gaps).
    *   Signage should have logical spacing and be limited to a maximum of four signs.
    *   A "whereas" statement must be included to explain the reason for the specific name.
*   **Cost & Funding:** The estimated cost for a set of four signs (including manufacturing and installation) is approximately $800 (roughly $200 per sign). It was agreed that all costs must be borne by the applicant or a sponsoring organization; no city funds will be used for this purpose.
*   **Duration:** There was discussion regarding "sunsetting" these names (e.g., a 10-year limit) rather than making them permanent, similar to the practices used in neighboring Minneapolis.

**Public Safety & Quality of Life:**
*   **Emergency Response:** A significant concern was raised regarding the potential for confusion for emergency responders (Police/Fire/Dispatch). The committee discussed ensuring that co-names do not interfere with the ability of first responders to quickly identify locations.
*   **Infrastructure Priorities:** There was a noted concern that the city's resources (both staff time and public attention) should be prioritized toward critical infrastructure, such as addressing copper wire theft, improving street lighting, and repairing road markings.

**Points of Contention:**
*   **The Role of the Heritage Preservation Commission (HPC):** There was a debate regarding whether the HPC is the appropriate body to review co-naming applications. While some suggested using them as a standard gatekeeper, others argued that because co-naming is a "short-form" honorary gesture rather than a permanent legal renaming, it may fall outside the HPC's specific mandate for building environments.
*   **Overall Necessity:** Not all members agreed on resuming the practice. Some members expressed that since the primary goal of co-naming is a "short" honorary sign, the city should be cautious about creating new administrative layers to manage it, especially when there are significant infrastructure and safety issues to address.
