#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Policy Committee Meeting  
**Purpose:** To discuss and develop Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) regarding the re-introduction of honorary street co-naming.  
**Date/Time:** October 21, 2026, at 1:00 PM.  
**Context:** The city stopped the practice of co-naming streets in 2018 due to concerns over signage clarity and public safety. This meeting served as a "workshop" to determine how to potentially resume the practice with clear rules and oversight.

#### Summary
The committee focused on the logistics, costs, and administrative hurdles involved in allowing honorary co-names on city streets. Key areas of discussion included:

*   **Proposed Procedures and Costs:**
    *   Public Works outlined technical requirements for new co-namings, including the need for continuous road segments, logical spacing between signs, and a maximum of four signs per co-naming.
    *   **Budget/Cost:** It was noted that the cost for a set of four signs (including production and installation) is approximately **$775** (roughly $200 per sign). 
    *   **Funding:** There is a push to ensure that all costs associated with these signs are borne by the petitioner or a sponsoring organization rather than the city's general fund.

*   **Public Safety and Quality of Life:**
    *   A significant portion of the discussion revolved around the potential for confusion. The goal is to ensure that co-names do not interfere with emergency response or standard navigation.
    *   There was a recommendation to include a "sunset" clause (possibly 10 years) for some co-names to manage long-term maintenance and avoid "clutter" on the streets.

*   **Areas of Contention & Disagreement:**
    *   **Administrative Oversight:** There was a notable debate regarding which city body should oversee the process. While one proposal suggested using the Heritage Preservation Commission (HPC), officials from the Department of Environment (PED) expressed concern that the HPC’s current mandate is focused on building environments and may not be the appropriate venue for "honorary" naming, which is a different legal and logistical matter than permanent street renaming.
    *   **Resource Allocation:** A significant point of contention arose regarding city priorities. Council Member Jost expressed strong opposition to the co-naming program, arguing that city resources and staff time should instead be directed toward critical infrastructure and safety issues, such as copper wire theft, street lighting, and repainting worn road markings.
    *   **Policy Integrity:** There were discussions regarding the need for an objective, third-party review process to ensure that naming decisions are based on merit rather than political influence, and that all applicants undergo a thorough vetting process for their historical's public records.

**Current Status:** No final vote was taken during this meeting. The session was used to gather information and evaluate the two competing proposals (from Council Members Bowie and Johnson) to eventually form a unified resolution for the SOPs.
