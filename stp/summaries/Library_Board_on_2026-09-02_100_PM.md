#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Library Board Meeting
**Purpose:** Review and discussion of the 2027 Library Budget proposal.
**Date/Time:** September 2, 2026, at 1:00 PM.
**Context:** The meeting focused on the Mayor’s proposed budget, specifically regarding the potential closure of the Dayton's Bluff branch, the funding of safety personnel, and the management of the library's fund balance.

#### Summary
The meeting centered on the 2027 budget, with several key points regarding operations, safety, and the future of specific locations.

**Budget and Operations:**
*   **Fee Increases:** The library is proposing an increase in fees for certain services: black and white copies/prints will increase from $0.15 to $0.20 per page, and notary services will increase from $1.00 to $2.00 per transaction.
*   **New Technology:** There is a proposed one-time investment to replace the current manual "clicker" system with real-time technology to track visitor counts by location, hour, and day of the week.
*   **Library Materials:** There was a noted concern regarding the declining base budget for library materials and the need to ensure purchasing power stays consistent with inflation.

**Public Safety and Staffing:**
*   **Safety Specialists:** The budget currently faces a multi-year funding challenge for four of the seven library safety specialists. 
*   **The "Trade-off":** The current proposal "couples" the cost savings from the potential closure of the Dayton's Bluff Library (specifically by ending the lease and reducing staff costs) with the funding for these safety positions.

**Controversy and Disagreement:**
The most significant area of contention involves the **proposed closure of the Dayton's Bluff Library**. 
*   **The "Usage" Argument:** While the budget suggests closure based on lower usage numbers compared to other branches, Councilmember Johnson and others argued that this data is misleading. They pointed out that Dayton's Bluff is a smaller, leased space with fewer hours of operation and serves a disadvantaged neighborhood. They argued that comparing it to larger, city-owned branches is "comparing apples to oranges."
*   **The Fund Balance:** There was a heated exchange regarding the library's "fund balance" (approximately $9 million). Councilmember Johnson pointed out that the library frequently under-spends its budget and suggested that rather than closing a neighborhood branch to solve a "budget problem," the city should look at the existing fund balance or other funding streams to maintain the branch and fund safety specialists.
*   **Community Impact:** Councilmember Yang expressed strong opposition to the closure, noting that the neighborhood has no other nearby alternatives and that the library is a vital resource for lower-income and marginalized residents.

Ultimately, there was a general consensus that the board is not eager to close the Dayton's Bluff branch, but they must still find a viable way to secure the multi-year funding required for safety staff and other operational needs.
