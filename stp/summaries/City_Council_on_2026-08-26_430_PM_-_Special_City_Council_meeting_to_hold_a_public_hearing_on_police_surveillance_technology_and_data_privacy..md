#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Special City Council Meeting / Public Hearing
**Purpose:** To hold a public hearing on police surveillance technology and data privacy.
**Date/Time:** August 26, 2026, at 4:30 PM.
**Context:** The hearing was designed to gather public input regarding the city's use of and contracts with specific surveillance technologies, including automated license plate readers (ALPR) and drones.

#### Summary
The meeting was dominated by public testimony regarding the city's contracts with surveillance companies, specifically **Flock**, **Axon**, and **Skydio**. While the council did not take a final vote on the contracts during this specific hearing, the session served as a platform for significant public outcry and opposition to these technologies.

**Key Issues and Concerns:**
*   **Data Privacy and Safety:** Numerous residents testified that these systems create a "mass surveillance" network. Concerns were raised that the technology allows for "pre-crime" and "predictive policing," which critics argue infringes on Fourth Amendment rights. There was a strong emphasis on the fact that these tools are often used by law enforcement to target protesters, stalk individuals, and facilitate immigration enforcement.
*   **Lack of Transparency:** A major point of contention was the lack of public notice regarding the procurement of these tools. Citizens reported that some contracts were entered into without the knowledge of the council or the public. There were calls for the city to disclose the specific terms and budgets associated with the Skydio and Flock programs.
*   **Contractual Morality:** A significant portion of the testimony focused on the ethical implications of the city’s business ties. Many residents demanded the cancellation of contracts with Skydio and Axon, citing their involvement in international conflicts and their use in military-grade surveillance.
*   **Technical Reliability:** Concerns were raised regarding the inaccuracy of automated systems. Testimonies highlighted high error rates in license plate recognition (reportedly as high as 1 in 10), which could lead to the harassment of innocent citizens based on faulty data.

**Highlights of Contention:**
The public testimony was overwhelmingly critical of the current surveillance infrastructure. The primary areas of disagreement were:
*   **Public Safety vs. Surveillance:** While proponents of the tech suggest it aids in crime prevention, many residents countered that the technology creates an "open-air prison" and offers a "false sense of security" while compromising civil liberties.
*   **Privacy Rights:** There was a heavy focus on how these systems allow for warrantless searches of personal data, which many residents felt violated the city's status as a sanctuary for its inhabitants.

The hearing concluded with the Council acknowledging the extensive public input. No immediate vote was taken, but the Council stated they will process the testimony as they move forward with decisions regarding these contracts.
