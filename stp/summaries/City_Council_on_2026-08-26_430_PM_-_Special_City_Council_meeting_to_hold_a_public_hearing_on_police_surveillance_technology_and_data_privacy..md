#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Special City Council Meeting (Public Hearing)
**Purpose:** To hold a public hearing regarding police surveillance technology and data privacy.
**Date/Time:** August 26, 2026, at 4:30 PM.

#### Summary
The meeting was dedicated entirely to a public hearing where residents shared their concerns and testimonies regarding the city's use of high-tech surveillance tools. The primary focus was on the risks posed by automated systems and the lack of transparency regarding the contracts and funding associated with them.

**Key Technologies and Concerns:**
*   **Flock (Automatic License Plate Readers - ALPR):** Residents expressed strong opposition to Flock technology, citing concerns that it is "made for abuse" and allows for the tracking of individuals without warrants. There is significant concern regarding the sharing of this data with third parties and federal agencies, particularly for immigration enforcement. Many speakers noted that the system is prone to inaccuracies and high-volume "false positives."
*   **Skydio (Drones):** There was significant contention regarding the city’s contracts with Skydio. Residents highlighted that the technology is "militarized" and argued that the city is currently hiding the specific details of these contracts, including the total amount of money being spent. Several residents linked the use of these drones to concerns over civil liberties and the city's standing as a sanctuary city.
*   **Axon & AI-Powered Systems:** There is a broad call from the community to move beyond just removing specific brands and instead implement a blanket ban on all "AI-powered mass surveillance" and any tools that allow for the "bundling" of personal data.

**Public Safety and Quality of Life:**
*   Many residents argued that surveillance does not equal safety. They expressed that these tools create a "dystopian" atmosphere and can be used to target protestors, private individuals, and marginalized communities.
*   Several residents raised concerns about the "over-policing" of certain neighborhoods and how data-driven tools can reinforce existing biases.

**Budget and Transparency:**
*   There were several complaints regarding the lack of transparency in the procurement process. Residents expressed frustration that these contracts were expanded without public input or clear disclosure of the budget allocations.
*   A recurring demand from the public was for the City Council to release the full, unredacted contracts with Flock, Axon, and Skydio to ensure the community knows exactly what data is being collected and who it is being shared with.

**Notable Moments of Contention:**
While the council's vote to close the hearing was unanimous, the testimony from the public highlighted a deep and unified community opposition to the current trajectory of surveillance technology. The primary point of contention is the discrepancy between the city's stated goals of safety and the reality of what the public perceives as a "war on privacy." Residents are calling for the council to act as "stewards of our money" and ethics by severing ties with companies and technologies that they view as intrusive and morally problematic.
