#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Special City Council Meeting (Public Hearing)
**Purpose:** To hold a public hearing regarding police surveillance technology and data privacy.
**Date/Time:** August 26, 2026, at 4:30 PM.
**Context:** The meeting was dedicated specifically to gathering public testimony on the use of Automated License Plate Readers (ALPR), AI-powered surveillance, and drone technology (specifically focusing on systems from providers such as Flock, Axon, and Skydio).

#### Summary
The meeting served as a public hearing where numerous residents and community members testified regarding the city's use of high-tech surveillance. While the council did not take a final vote on specific contract changes during this hearing, the testimony provided a clear and heavy consensus from the public regarding the risks of these technologies.

**Key Issues & Concerns:**
*   **Legal and Regulatory Violations:** Residents pointed out that state statutes already regulate ALPR use, requiring it to be tied to active criminal investigations. There were concerns that current contracts with companies like Flock may violate these state laws by creating large, searchable databases.
*   **Privacy & "Dystopian" Surveillance:** A significant number of residents voiced concerns that the integration of AI, drones, and large-scale data tracking is creating an "omnipresent" surveillance network. Many argued that these tools do not necessarily increase safety but instead create a "panopticon" effect, potentially leading to the erosion of civil liberties.
*   **Accuracy & Reliability:** Several speakers highlighted the "black box" nature of AI algorithms, noting that these systems are often non-deterministic and prone to high error rates. Specific instances were cited where automated systems misidentified vehicles or individuals, leading to potential for harassment or wrongful enforcement.
*   **Impact on Sanctuary Status:** A major point of contention was the intersection of surveillance and immigration. Residents argued that these technologies (particularly Flock) are easily accessible by ICE. Several speakers expressed that the city’s use of these tools undermines St. Paul’s status as a sanctuary city and puts immigrant neighbors at risk.
*   **Ethical and International Concerns:** A large portion of the testimony focused on the specific companies involved. Many residents called for the city to sever ties with Skydio and other providers due to their ties to international military operations and "techno-fascist" entities.

**Budget and Transparency:**
*   **Transparency:** There was significant frustration regarding the lack of transparency in current contracts. Some residents claimed that certain drone programs were expanded without public oversight or clear budget disclosures.
*   **Cost vs. Value:** Residents questioned the efficacy of spending tax dollars on "militarized" technology, suggesting that funds would be better spent on community resources and local jobs.

**Key Decisions/Status:**
The meeting concluded with a motion to close the public hearing. While no final vote on the cancellation of specific contracts occurred during this specific session, the council acknowledged the substantial volume of testimony regarding the necessity of moving toward more transparent, community-focused, and less intrusive surveillance policies.
