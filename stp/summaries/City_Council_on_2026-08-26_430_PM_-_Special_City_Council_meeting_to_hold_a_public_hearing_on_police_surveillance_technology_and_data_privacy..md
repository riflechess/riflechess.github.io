#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Special City Council Meeting
**Purpose:** Public hearing on police surveillance technology and data privacy.
**Date/Time:** August 26, 2026, at 4:30 PM.
**Context:** The council held a dedicated session to allow citizens to provide testimony regarding the use of automated license plate readers (ALPR), drones, and AI-powered surveillance tools within the city.

#### Summary
The primary focus of this meeting was a public hearing regarding the city's use of, and contracts with, specific surveillance technologies. While the council did not take a final vote on a specific resolution during this session, the testimony provided by residents was overwhelmingly critical of current and proposed surveillance programs.

**Key Issues & Technologies:**
*   **Flock (Automated License Plate Readers):** Residents voiced significant concerns regarding the "Flock" system. Key complaints included its potential for use in stalking, the inaccuracy of its data, and the fact that it provides a "backdoor" for immigration enforcement (ICE). There is also concern that these contracts may violate state statutes regarding how ALPR data is shared and stored.
*   **Skydio (Drones):** Testimony regarding Skydio drones focused on a lack of transparency regarding the city's contracts. Residents expressed strong opposition to the "automated" nature of drone surveillance and were particularly vocal about the ethical implications of using hardware produced by companies with ties to international conflicts.
*   **Axon & AI Integration:** Several speakers called for a broad ban on any "AI-powered mass surveillance" and the tools that facilitate "pre-crime" or "predictive policing" analytics.

**Public Safety & Quality of Life:**
A recurring theme in the testimony was the distinction between "safety" and "surveillance." Residents argued that these technologies do not necessarily make the community safer but do create a "panopticon" effect that erodes privacy and can lead to the targeting of protesters and marginalized communities. There were specific concerns that these tools are being used as "pre-emptive" measures that could lead to the wrongful identification of residents.

**Budget and Transparency:**
Several residents highlighted the lack of public oversight in how these contracts were secured. There were specific demands for the city to disclose the full extent of the spending on Skydio and Flock, with some residents noting that city funds are being directed toward these systems while other public services (such as libraries) face budget cuts.

**Contention & Public Sentiment:**
The sentiment from the public was nearly unanimous in its opposition to current surveillance contracts. The primary points of contention were:
*   The perceived erosion of St. Paul’s status as a "sanctuary city" due to the accessibility of data by federal immigration agencies.
*   The ethical implications of the city’s business relationships with specific tech providers.
*   The lack of transparency regarding how much the city is paying for these "advanced" technologies versus their actual efficacy in preventing crime.

The session concluded with the Council acknowledging the testimony and advising residents to contact their individual council members to discuss the next steps in drafting ordinances regarding these technologies.
