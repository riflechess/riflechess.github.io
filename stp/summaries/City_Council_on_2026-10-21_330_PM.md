#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting
**Purpose:** Monthly legislative business, including resolution approvals, land use, and public safety initiatives.
**Date/Time:** October 21, 2026, at 3:30 PM

#### Summary
The Council addressed several items concerning community development, public safety, and infrastructure funding. Key highlights include:

**Public Safety & Quality of Life**
*   **Homelessness Response:** The Council approved a three-phased resolution to address unsheltered homelessness. The plan includes a 60-day strategic investment in "navigation" before any encampment closures, the creation of a six-month low-barrier shelter, and the development of a permanent "indoor village" model. While the council reached a unanimous vote, there was extensive discussion regarding the urgency of the situation and the need for robust partnerships with community providers.
*   **Domestic Violence Awareness:** Resolution 26-1770 was passed to declare October 2026 as Domestic Violence Awareness Month.
*   **Property Condemnation (855 3rd Street East):** The council reviewed a case regarding a property deemed unfit for human habitation. A tenant requested an extension until January; however, the Council moved to a November 10, 2026, deadline for all necessary repairs to be completed. If not met, the property will be declared unfit for human habitation, bypassing standard eviction proceedings.

**Business & Development**
*   **West 7th Arts District:** A $25,000 grant was approved to fund branding and the installation of 15 "color the corridor" banners to establish the district’s identity.
*   **Snelling and University Intersection:** The Council approved "Version 2" of a new commercial development district. This version was specifically crafted to address community concerns by limiting the number of available liquor licenses while still allowing for the growth of small businesses and local vendors.
*   **Carpenters' Wages:** An ordinance was moved to align local rules with state statutes, allowing for federal residential prevailing wage rates for carpenters on certain city-financed wood frame projects.

**Budget & Infrastructure**
*   **Grant Acquisitions:** Several grants were accepted for city improvements:
    *   **Safe Streets for All:** A major federal/state grant of $14 million.
    *   **Park Improvements:** Various grants were approved for the Dousman Park project ($50k), Point Douglas regional trail ($200k), and the Merriam Park Skate Park ($500k).
    *   **City Services:** Funds were allocated for enhanced street sweeping in the Ramsey-Washington-Metro District.
*   **Community Events:** A noise variance was granted for a "Turkey Trot" event on November 26, 2026.
