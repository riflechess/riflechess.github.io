#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting
**Purpose:** General City Business, Appointments, and Public Hearings
**Date/Time:** November 18, 2026, at 3:30 PM
**Context:** This meeting included several significant appointments for city leadership and public safety, as well as discussions on and hearings for various city ordinances and federal grants.

#### Summary

**Personnel & Appointments**
*   **Parks and Recreation Commission:** The Council unanimously approved the appointment of Adelyn Tao and Lizbeth Martinez-Mota to two-year terms.
*   **Fire Department Leadership:** In a significant move for public safety, the Council unanimously appointed Jeremiah Melquist as the new Chief of the St. Paul Fire Department. The appointment received strong support from both council members and fire department leadership.

**Public Safety & City Policy**
*   **Mobile Saunas:** The Council unanimously approved an ordinance to allow mobile saunas in city parks. 
*   **Garbage Service:** A new ordinance was adopted to update the procedure and set rates for base-level garbage service, effective January 1, 2027.
*   **Administrative Citations:** The first reading of an ordinance to establish procedures for civil penalties for city ordinance violations was held. This will be laid over until early January 2027 to allow for further community feedback and staff review.
*   **Sustainable Building Ordinance:** The first reading for an update to the Sustainable Building Ordinance was held. The updated ordinance aims to simplify requirements for smaller projects and update eligibility standards.

**Zoning & Property**
*   **Rice Street Rezoning:** A request to rezone property at 1201 Rice Street from a T2 (Traditional Neighborhood) to a B3 (General Business) district was **denied**. The council noted that the current zoning better supports transit-oriented, multi-use development.
*   **Fence Variance (775 Edmond Avenue):** A request for a variance to increase the height of a privacy fence was heard. Due to the complexity of the measurements and the need for precise wording regarding the height, the matter was laid over until December 2.

**Controversial & Non-Unanimous Votes**
*   **Police Commission Stipends (Item 38):** This vote was **not unanimous**. While the goal of increasing stipends for the Police Civilian Internal Affairs Review Commission was supported, Councilmember Johnson expressed concern regarding the specific wording of the ordinance. She argued that the current draft allows for a lack of transparency by giving the "HERO" director (a currently vacant position) the discretion to set the amount. The item was laid over to January 6, 2027.
*   **CCTV & Records Management Grants (Items 42, 43, and 44):** These items received significant public pushback. Residents expressed strong concerns regarding "DoJ" funding, "Axon" contracts, and the potential for increased surveillance and AI-enabled technology. 
    *   **Item 42 (JAG Grant):** This vote was **not unanimous**. Councilmember Kim expressed the need for more information regarding the specific costs associated with "outside agencies" before a final decision could be made.
    *   **Items 43 & 44:** These were **laid over** until December 2. The council sought to provide a clearer breakdown of how the federal funds would be spent to ensure they were only used for maintaining existing systems rather than expanding surveillance capabilities.

**Additional Approvals**
*   The Council approved several resolutions to accept federal and local grants for K-9 equipment, park improvements, and fire department reimbursements.
*   A variety of administrative and clarity-related ordinances were passed unanimously.
