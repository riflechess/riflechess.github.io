#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting
**Purpose:** Regular Council Meeting
**Date/Time:** November 18, 2026, at 3:30 PM
**Context:** Includes several appointments, ordinance votes, and public hearings regarding public safety and local infrastructure.

#### Summary

**Appointments & Leadership**
*   **New Appointments:** The Council unanimously approved several new appointments, including members for the Parks and Recreation Commission, the Mayor’s Advisory Council for People with Disabilities, and the selection of **Jeremiah Melquist as the new Chief of the Fire Department**. The appointment of the new Fire Chief was highly praised by the council for his experience and commitment to the city.
*   **Parks and Recreation:** The council approved a new ordinance allowing **mobile saunas** to be placed in city parks, catering to a growing local interest in winter amenities.

**Zoning & Development**
*   **Rice Street Rezoning (Denied):** A proposal to rezone 1201 Rice Street from T2 to B3 was **denied**. The council determined that the current zoning is better suited to support transit-oriented development and walkability, whereas the B3 zoning would favor a higher number of auto-related businesses.
*   **Garbage Services:** An ordinance was adopted to update the operational procedures and set rates for base-level garbage service starting in early 2027.
*   **Sustainable Building Ordinance:** The first reading of an update to the Sustainable Building Ordinance was held. The goal is to provide more flexibility for smaller projects (under $500,000) while maintaining high standards for large developments.

**Public Safety & Technology (Controversial Items)**
There was significant discussion and public testimony regarding several federal grants for police technology. These items were **not approved immediately** and were moved to a December 2nd hearing to allow for more transparency.
*   **CCTV and Records Management (Items 42, 43, 44):** These items involve accepting federal grants to improve CCTV systems and upgrade the Records Management System (RMS). 
*   **The Conflict:** Public testimony included strong opposition to "over-surveillance" and the use of AI-enabled technology. Residents and some council members expressed concerns regarding the "Axon" contract, specifically that it is a "bundled" contract making it difficult to see how much of the budget goes to specific features like cloud storage or data sharing.
*   **The Resolution:** Because the council wanted more clarity on how the money would be spent and a better understanding of the "Ax1on" contract's scope, they opted to delay these votes to ensure they have a clear "itemized" look at the contract before proceeding.

**Community & Local Issues**
*   **Police Stipends (Item 38):** There was a **split vote (5-1)** regarding the funding for the Police Civilian Internal Affairs Review Commission (PCARC). While the council supports the intent to increase stipends for these commissioners, one council member expressed concern that the proposed language was not transparent enough regarding how these stipends would be calculated annually.
*   **Fence Variances (Items 48, 50, 51):** Several items regarding fence heights and properties on Dale Street and Elm Avenue were heard. These involved disputes between neighbors regarding privacy and property lines. Most of these were moved to December to allow for further inspection and clarity on the safety of the structures.
