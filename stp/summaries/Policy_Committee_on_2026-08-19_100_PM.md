#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Policy Committee Meeting  
**Purpose:** Discussion regarding Automated Vehicles (AVs) with representatives from the Minnesota Department of Transportation (MnDOT).  
**Date/Time:** August 19, 2026, at 1:00 PM  
**Context:** MnDOT and local officials discussed the current state of AV technology, existing and potential legislation in Minnesota, safety impacts, and the effect on the workforce.

#### Summary
The meeting focused on preparing for the integration of automated vehicles into the transportation system, highlighting both the opportunities for innovation and the logistical challenges regarding safety and labor.

**Public Safety & Quality of Life**
*   **Safety Benefits:** Officials noted that over 90% of traffic accidents are caused by human factors. There is a strong interest in whether AV technology can reduce the high number of annual fatalities on Minnesota roads. One cited study indicated a significantly lower crash rate for Waymo vehicles compared to human drivers through 2024.
*   **Accessibility:** While AVs offer potential to improve mobility for those who cannot drive, it was noted that current systems still require a human operator to manage tasks like wheelchair securement.
*   **Safety Concerns:** There were discussions regarding the risks of "deadheading" (unoccupied vehicles on the road) and ensuring safety in extreme weather conditions common in Minnesota.

**State of Legislation & Infrastructure**
*   **Current Law:** Currently, Minnesota has no specific laws that explicitly prohibit or authorize fully autonomous vehicles. This creates a "silent" period where various interpretations may exist regarding what is legal for companies like Waymo to operate locally.
*   **Infrastructure Costs:** MnDOT currently does not anticipate significant infrastructure spending to support AVs, as the technology is designed to run on existing roads (at least until vehicle volume reaches much higher scales).
*   **State Coordination:** The Governor’s Advisory Council on Connected Automated Vehicles will continue to develop policy recommendations for 2027 and beyond.

**Economic Impact & Workforce (Areas of Contention)**
The most significant areas of discussion involved the potential impact on human workers:
*   **Job Displacement:** There is a clear concern regarding the loss of jobs for delivery drivers, freight operators, and TNC (Transportation Network Company) drivers. 
*   **Equity Concerns:** Council members highlighted that these job losses would disproportionately affect immigrant communities and people of color who currently make up a large portion of the local transit and taxi workforce.
*   **Mitigation:** While some see AVs as an opportunity for growth, others view them as a direct threat to stable employment. The city and state are looking into ways to partner with agencies like DEED to plan for these transitions.

**Budget & Administration**
*   While significant infrastructure costs aren't expected immediately, there will be administrative costs at the state level regarding licensing, registration, and permit management. Potential revenue from permit fees may be used to offset these operational costs.
