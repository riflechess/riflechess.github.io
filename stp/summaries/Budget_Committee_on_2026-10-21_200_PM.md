#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Budget Committee Meeting
**Purpose:** Review of the five-year capital plan (2027–2031)
**Date/Time:** October 21, 2026, at 2:00 PM
**Context:** The committee met with the Department of Public Works to review draft plans for upcoming infrastructure, funding sources, and coordination with state and federal partners.

#### Summary
The meeting focused primarily on the **2027–2031 five-year capital plan**. While still in the draft phase, the presentation provided a roadmap for infrastructure spending, funding sources, and upcoming projects.

**Funding Sources**
The city utilizes several distinct funding streams to manage infrastructure:
*   **RSVP Program:** Bond-funded for residential reconstruction and mill/overlay work.
*   **Municipal State Aid:** Derived from the gas tax; primarily used for arterial roads.
*   **Common Sense Fund:** Dedicated to specific "common-sense" corridors.
*   **CIB Funds & General Funds:** Used for smaller projects like sidewalk improvements and local enhancements.
*   **External Sources:** Federal, State, and County funds (highly dependent on specific eligibility and competitive processes).
*   **Transportation Advancement Account (TAA):** Used for local shares and engineering where general funds are insufficient.

**Infrastructure & Public Safety Highlights**
Significant focus was placed on projects improving public safety and the "quality of life" for pedestrians and cyclists:
*   **Safety Initiatives:** Several "Safe Routes to School" and "non-motorized safety" projects were highlighted, including the Payne Avenue corridor, Chelsea Heights, and Jefferson Avenue.
*   **Recent Wins:** The Kellogg Third Street Bridge is nearly complete (ribbon-cutting scheduled for the weekend of the meeting), and the Kellogg Capital City Bikeway is nearing completion near the Grand Casino Arena.
*   **Upcoming Critical Work:** The Vandalia commercial area requires significant attention due to heavy wear from commercial activity. Additionally, several alley improvements were completed to address drainage and safety risks.
*   **Delayed Projects:** The St. Anthony project was pushed to next year due to complexities surrounding a cyber attack and proximity to freeway access.

**Advocacy and Coordination**
A significant portion of the discussion involved the city's role in seeking state and federal funding:
*   **State Advocacy:** The city is actively lobbying the state for funds for massive projects, such as the West 7th Corridor (a $100+ million project).
*   **Partnership Challenges:** There was a discussion regarding the complexity of "cumulative" construction impacts. The city works to coordinate with water, sewer, and private utilities (like telecom) to minimize the time residents are inconvenienced by heavy construction.
*   **Federal Delays:** It was noted that federal funding cycles have faced delays due to changing eligibility criteria, which impacts the timing of some large-scale projects.

**Key Discussion Points**
While the meeting was generally collaborative, specific attention was given to the **uncertainty of timing** for projects in the later years of the five-year plan (2029–2031). Because these projects rely on competitive federal grants and multi-agency coordination, their exact dates are "squishy" until funding is secured. Council members also pushed for more clarity on how the city manages "pre-determined" costs like lighting and railroad crossings to ensure those funds are not diverted.
