#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Library Board Meeting  
**Purpose:** Review of budget amendments, community projects, and facility updates.  
**Date/Time:** November 4, 2026, at 1:00 PM.  
**Context:** City of Saint Paul Library Board proceedings.

#### Summary
**Budget and Expenditures**
*   **Travel Grant:** The board unanimously approved a gift from the Digital Public Library of America to cover travel and lodging for Library Director Maureen Hartman to attend a conference in Seattle in mid-November.
*   **Construction Funding:** The board approved a budget amendment to accept $1 million from the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). This funding is earmarked for the reconstruction of both the Hayden Heights and Riverview libraries.

**Community Outreach**
*   **Timberwolves Special Edition Card:** The board discussed the launch of a "Timberwolves" edition library card featuring artwork by local St. Paul artist Delia Touche. The card aims to increase new memberships and celebrate local culture. There are 7,000 cards in circulation, and a similar card for the Lynx is expected next year.

**Facility Updates (Rondo Community Library)**
*   **Relocation:** The Rondo Community Library is currently operating out of the Hallie Q. Brown Community Center. It is scheduled to move back to its permanent location at 461 Dale Street on December 31, 2026.
*   **Renovations:** Significant upgrades to the 461 Dale Street site include moved bathrooms, more study rooms, updated technology (AV equipment, high-speed Wi-Fi, and more power outlets), and a new public art mural in the entryway.
*   **Public Safety:** The report highlighted that the move to the Dale Street location includes a dedicated safety plan involving 12-13 staff members, two safety specialists, and ongoing coordination with the Western District Police Department to ensure a secure environment.

**Points of Discussion/Clarification**
*   **Artistic Procurement:** There was a specific discussion regarding the selection criteria for the mural artist at the Rondo location. Councilmember Bowie sought clarification on the distinction between an artist having "ties" to the Rondo/Frogtown area versus being a "resident" of the area. The discussion emphasized the importance of ensuring that city funds for public art specifically support and reinvest in local neighborhood artists.
