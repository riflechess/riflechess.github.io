#### Meeting Info
- **Type:** Budget Committee Meeting
- **Purpose:** Review and discussion of the 2027 proposed budget, specifically focusing on the Office of Financial Services (OFS), climate action initiatives, debt management, and capital improvements.
- **Date/Time:** October 14, 2026, at 10:00 AM.

#### Summary
**Office of Financial Services (OFS) & General Budget**
The OFS presented its goals for 2027, which include improving payroll/benefits administration and addressing a backlog of deferred maintenance. Key updates include:
*   **Personnel:** Addition of an accountant position to manage payroll and benefits.
*   **Police Support:** A "net zero" transfer of festival and event funding to the St. Paul Police Department was noted.
*   **Successes:** The city secured $8.5 million in federally directed spending and invested $3 million into a new solid waste fleet (22 vehicles/pieces of equipment).
*   **Debt Management:** The city’s AAA bond rating was maintained, reflecting strong financial management. However, a significant $3.7 million hit to the debt service fund is expected this year, partially due to a judgment bond (a legal settlement) that will be paid off over a six-year period.

**Climate Action & Resilience Plan (CARP)**
The committee discussed the integration of climate goals into the OFS budget:
*   **Funding:** Out of approximately $29 million in projected revenue from a new Xcel Energy franchise agreement, $500,000 is being initially allocated for 2027.
*   **EV/Scooter Program:** There was significant discussion regarding the "crisis" of the current electric bike/scooter program. Due to a budget gap and an expiring contract, the city is currently focused on securing and moving all owned units to secure locations while seeking new operators.
*   **Climate Support:** A new "Climate Infrastructure and Response Fund" was proposed to provide a dedicated source of funding for matching federal/state grants and supporting ongoing climate-related subscriptions and tools.

**Debt and Sales Tax**
The committee reviewed the 0.5% and 1.0% sales tax structures:
*   **Roy Wilkins Arena:** Approximately $40 million is being budgeted toward this project, with the city seeking additional state funds to cover the remainder of the construction.
*   **Neighborhood Star Programs:** The budget includes increases for various community programs and capital improvements.
*   **Infrastructure:** Discussion included the transition of several high-cost items (like sewer and water infrastructure) toward more sustainable funding models.

**Public Safety & Community Impact**
*   **Opioid Response:** There is a $1 million allocation specifically for fentanyl response. 
*   **Inter-Agency Collaboration:** There is an ongoing effort to coordinate with Ramsey County on the distribution of opioid settlement funds, specifically regarding Suboxone programs and treatment facilities. 

**Points of Contention & Detail**
*   **EV Scooter Program Collapse:** This was a point of notable concern. The loss of the current provider was described as a "not-preferred" outcome that required quick action to secure city-owned assets. There is a push for more robust long-term planning and clearer communication regarding these funds to ensure they are not "lost" during the transition to new operators.
*   **Opioid Coordination:** There were discussions regarding the need for a more "coherent" and "coordinated" strategy between the City and the County to ensure the $1 million opioid settlement funds are used effectively, particularly in high-need areas like Ward 1.
*   **Sales Tax Structure:** There was some inquiry regarding the "roundabout" way the current budget ensures compliance with state-mandated formulas for the 60/40 split of sales tax funds; however, it was clarified that the current plan stays within legal requirements.
