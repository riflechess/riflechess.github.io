#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting
**Purpose:** Discussion and vote on city ordinances, resolutions regarding budget allocations, and public hearings on local services.
**Date/Time:** August 5, 2026, at 3:30 PM

#### Summary

**Tenant Protection & Ballot Initiative (Non-Unanimous Vote)**
The council debated a resolution to put a tenant protection ordinance on the ballot via a petition from Safe Homes St. Paul. This item saw significant contention and ultimately failed with a **2-5 vote**. 
*   **Arguments in Favor:** Proponents argued that allowing the initiative provides an important democratic outlet for residents and highlighted that similar ordinances have successfully passed in other cities like Duluth. They emphasized the importance of tenant rights for the city's high percentage of renters.
*   **Arguments Against:** Opponents expressed concerns regarding legal risks. They noted that the current wording was deemed "unlawful" by some experts and could be seen as a conflict with state law or even the U.S. Constitution. There were concerns that passing an unenforceable ordinance would mislead voters and leave the city unable to act for at least a year.

**Public Safety & Fire/Police Funding**
Several items regarding public safety were approved unanimously:
*   **Fire Department:** Secured $360,000 for hazardous materials response (joint powers agreement with the State), $500,000 for training and equipment for the Minnesota Air Rescue Team, and approximately $21,131 in reimbursements from the City of Edina.
*   **Police Department:** Accepted a $35,000 donation from the St. Paul Police Foundation and $25,000 for a port security grant.
*   **Other Safety Items:** A resolution was passed to include $50,000 in grant funding for two new curbside EV charging stations.

**Labor Relations & City Staffing**
A public hearing was held regarding the **Deputy City Clerk** position. 
*   **Public Testimony:** Union representatives (AFSCME) spoke strongly against making this an "unclassified" (non-union/appointed) role, arguing that it would strip a union job and create a precedent for reducing union representation in city departments.
*   **Status:** The matter was laid over until August 12 for final consideration after the public hearing.

**Economic Development & Community Quality of Life**
The council approved several items impacting local business and recreation:
*   **Golf Courses:** Ordinance 26-35 was adopted to allow "miniature golf" and permitted night usage (until 10 p.m.) for lit courses, specifically benefiting the Highland National Driving Range.
*   **Grants & Donations:** Significant funds were approved for the Commercial Corridors Program ($400k), a donation of $2.28 million to support the Como Zoo and Marjorie McNeely Conservatory, and various grants for trail connections.
*   **Sound Variances:** The city granted sound level variances for "India Fest 2026" and for the Wabasha Brewing Company's patio series.

**General Business & Regulations**
*   **Minimum Wage:** A move to eliminate the provisional 90-day minimum wage rate for workers aged 14–17 was laid over until August 12.
*   **Waste Management:** Ordinance 26-36 requires City Council approval for all mixed municipal solid waste contracts.
*   **Vacant Buildings:** A waiver was granted to a property owner on Arcade Street, allowing them 90 days to clear code violations before any further fees are assessed, in anticipation of the site becoming a cannabis grow facility.
