#### Meeting Info
**Type:** City Council Meeting
**Purpose:** Regular legislative session covering ordinances, resolutions, and public hearings.
**Date/Time:** August 5, 2026, at 3:30 PM
**Location:** Saint Paul, MN

#### Summary

**Tenant Protection Ballot Initiative (High Contention)**
The Council debated a resolution to place a tenant protection ordinance on the ballot as a petition-led initiative. This led to a significant divide among members:
*   **Arguments in Favor:** Supporters highlighted the importance of community engagement and the work of "Safe Homes St. Paul." They noted that similar language was successfully implemented in Duluth and argued that it is better to let the voters decide.
*   **Arguments Against:** Opponents, including several council members, voiced concerns regarding legal viability. They stated the ordinance was deemed "unlawful," a conflict of state law, potentially unconstitutional, and too vague to be enforced properly. There was significant concern that putting flawed language on the ballot would set voters up for failure.
*   **Result:** The motion failed with a 2-5 vote.

**Public Safety & Infrastructure (Budget/Grant Approvals)**
A series of resolutions were unanimously approved regarding public safety and city infrastructure:
*   **Fire & Rescue:** $360,000 was allocated for hazardous materials response; $500,000 was set aside for Minnesota Air Rescue Team training and equipment; and $21,131.47 was accepted as a reimbursement from the City of Edina.
*   **Police Department:** The city approved several budget amendments, including a $35,000 donation for the Police Foundation and a $25,000 grant for port security. 
*   **Public Works:** A $50,000 grant was accepted to purchase two curbside EV chargers.
*   **Parks & Recreation:** Large funds were approved for trail connections ($250,000), and a $2.28 million donation was accepted from "Como Friends" to support the zoo and conservatory.

**City Operations & Policy Changes**
*   **Golf Course Expansion:** An ordinance was adopted to modify rules regarding golf courses, specifically allowing for mini-golf and permitting lighted courses (such as the Highland National Driving Range) to operate until 10 p.m. to increase community access.
*   **Waste Management:** A new requirement was established for City Council approval of mixed municipal solid waste contracts.
*   **Minimum Wage:** The council heard a public hearing regarding the removal of a provisional 90-day minimum wage rate for workers aged 14–17; this matter is laid over until August 12th.

**Labor and Personnel (Public Hearing)**
A significant public hearing was held regarding the "Deputy City Clerk" position. Union representatives (AFSCME) provided heavy testimony against moving the role into the unclassified service. They argued that the move would eliminate a union job, they claim it is unnecessary to remove the role from the civil service to gain signing authority, and they characterized the move as "union busting." This matter is laid over until August 12th.

**Land Use & Small Business**
*   **Vacant Building Waiver:** The Council approved a recommendation for a 90-day waiver on vacant building fees for the property at 1047 Arcade Street, which the owner intends to use for a licensed cannabis grow operation.
*   **Sound Variances:** Two variances were approved for amplified sound: one for "India Fest 2026" and another for the Wabasha Brewing Company patio event series.
