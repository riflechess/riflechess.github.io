#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Housing & Redevelopment Authority (HRA) Meeting
**Purpose:** Discussion of TIF district decertifications, housing project feasibility, and community engagement.
**Date/Time:** October 7, 2026, at 2:00 PM
**Context:** City of Saint Paul.

#### Summary
**Housing Development & Community Engagement**
The meeting included a discussion regarding a construction project and the role of developer fees. A larger developer fee was noted as a method to secure tax credit basis, which helps the project's financial feasibility; these funds are eventually repaid via cash flow over a 15-year period. 

A significant point regarding **quality of life** was raised concerning the communication plan for current residents. There was a call for "rock solid" community engagement and "town hall style" communication to ensure residents are properly informed and prepared for the transition during the construction phase.

**TIF District Decertifications and Tax Impact**
The HRA is moving forward with actions regarding four specific TIF (Tax Increment Financing) districts. The goal is to decertify or amend these districts to move tax capacity back into the general tax base:
*   **Emerald Park:** Proposed to be closed early (ahead of its 2028 deadline).
*   **Pioneer Endicott:** Proposed to be closed much earlier (ahead of its 2040 deadline).
*   **Phelan Village & Riverfront Renaissance:** Proposed amendments to remove specific parcels from the TIF districts that no longer have tied debt obligations.

**Budget & Tax Impact**
*   The release of these tax capacities is estimated to result in a reduction of approximately **$10 in the median home’s tax burden** for the city's portion of the taxes.
*   While these actions reduce the tax burden on homeowners, they also reduce the amount of money "pooled" specifically for affordable housing initiatives.

**Points of Contention**
There was significant discussion regarding the **trade-off** between lowering property taxes and maintaining funds for affordable housing. While some members expressed gratitude for the potential reduction in property taxes, others raised concerns about the "financial tradeoff." 

The debate centered on the fact that by decertifying these TIF districts, the city is closing off specific funding avenues for affordable housing at a time when state and federal funding is scarce. Several high-priority projects (including Highland Bridge, the Heights Project, and the Hans Brewery redevelopment) were mentioned as needing significant funding, leading to a call for a deeper conversation on how the city will sustain affordable housing development in the future.

**Next Steps**
The formal vote on the four resolutions related to these TIF districts has been moved to the **November 18** meeting to allow for more time to review the impact on budgets and affordable housing resources.
