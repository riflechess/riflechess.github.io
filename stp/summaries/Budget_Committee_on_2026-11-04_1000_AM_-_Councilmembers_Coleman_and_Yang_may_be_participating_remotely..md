#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Budget Committee Meeting
**Purpose:** Review of the 2026 budget, specific departmental spending, and internal City Council office requests.
**Date/Time:** November 4, 2026, at 10:00 AM.
**Context:** The meeting included discussions regarding the "Hero" (Human Rights and Equity) department, the PCR commission, and administrative updates for the City Council's office.

#### Summary
The committee reviewed several key items regarding budget allocations, staffing, and infrastructure. Notable points of discussion and contention include:

**PCR Commission Stipends**
There was a discussion regarding the timing of the stipend review for the PCR commission. It was noted that the proposal was added to the agenda without extensive prior consultation with the council, leading to some frustration regarding the lack of transparency before it reached the public vote. The committee discussed the sensitivity of discussing individual stipends in a public forum.

**Human Rights and Equity (Hero) Department**
*   **Budget Status:** The department is currently running about 20% under budget due to several vacant positions.
*   **Director Vacancy:** A significant point of discussion was the **Director of Hero** position, which has remained vacant since 2022.
*   **Contention:** Councilmember Bowie expressed significant frustration regarding the lack of a clear timeline or plan from the Mayor’s office to fill this role. There was a debate regarding the practicality of continuing to "budget" for a high-salaried position (approximately $220,000) that remains unfilled for years without a clear roadmap for recruitment.

**City Council Office Requests**
The Director of Operations for the City Council, Jay Wilms, presented two specific funding requests:
*   **Office Renovation Repayment:** A request for $120,000 annually for three years (FY27–FY29) to repay an "interfund loan" used for office renovations, furniture, and construction.
*   **Administrative Citations:** A request for funding to hire consultants to modernize the technology and workflow for "administrative citations." This is intended to streamline how the city handles enforcement and penalties.

**Points of Contention regarding "Interfund Loans"**
There was notable friction regarding the use of "interfund loans" to cover renovation costs. Councilmember Bowie and others questioned the precedent of using this specific accounting tool and requested more clarity on how often this is used by other departments. The concern was whether this is a standard practice or a method to manage overspending without a clear, transparent strategy.

**Public Safety & Quality of Life**
The move toward "administrative citations" is a key item for the city's enforcement capabilities. The goal is to move away from manual, paper-based systems to a modernized, automated system to ensure that city rules and enforcement measures are handled efficiently.
