#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Public Safety Committee Meeting
**Purpose:** Reviewing diverse housing models and innovative solutions to address the city's unsheltered homeless population, encampments, and associated public safety concerns.
**Date/Time:** October 28, 2026, at 12:30 PM
**Context:** The committee received presentations from three different organizations/cities (Seattle, New Ulm, and Minneapolis/St. Cloud) to evaluate various housing infrastructure models as long-term alternatives to the current response to encampments.

#### Summary
The committee reviewed three distinct housing models designed to transition unsheltered individuals into stable living environments while improving public safety and quality of life.

**1. Tiny House Villages (Seattle Model)**
The Low Income Housing Institute presented the "Tiny House Village" model currently used in Seattle.
*   **Concept:** Small (8x12 ft) houses that provide a low-barrier entry point for various populations, including those with pets and those experiencing substance use disorders.
*   **Support Services:** Each site is staffed 24/7 by case managers and behavioral health specialists.
*   **Strategic Use of Land:** These sites are often used as "bridge" housing. The units are typically used for 3–5 years while the land is prepared for long-term development into permanent affordable housing.
*   **Cost/Scale:** While specific local costs were not finalized, the committee was interested in the cost-per-unit data to determine the feasibility of replicating the Seattle scale in St. Paul.

**2. Barnominiums (New Ulm Model)**
The City Manager of New Ulm presented the "Barnominium" model as a way to offer varied housing choices and better zoning management.
*   **Concept:** A hybrid of residential living and large-scale workshop/storage space (e.g., for mechanics, tradespeople, or those needing to store RVs).
*   **Community Benefit:** The goal is to move heavy equipment and RVs out of residential neighborhoods and into industrial-zoned areas.
*   **Financials:** The presentation included figures for building values (approximately $410,000 per unit) and noted that these units are often more affordable than traditional single-family homes because they provide both living and work space.
*   **TIF/Tax Incentives:** The discussion highlighted using tax abatements and TIFs to lower the cost of land and infrastructure, making these units more accessible for first-time homebuyers.

**3. Indoor Village Shelters (Avivo Model)**
Avivo presented a "trauma-informed" indoor model specifically for high-need populations.
*   **Target Audience:** This model is tailored for individuals with the highest "acuity" levels—those with serious mental health issues or substance use disorders who require 24/7 staffing.
*   **Features:** The "Four P's" (People, Pets, Privacy, and Possessions) are prioritized. Residents are provided with individual rooms to ensure dignity and safety.
*   **Cost Comparison:**
    *   **Seattle Model (Warehouse conversion):** Approximately $4 million for renovation; roughly $5 million in annual operating costs for 100 units.
    *   **St. Cloud Model (New construction):** Approximately $9 million for a 48-unit build; roughly $3.1 million in annual operating costs.

**Public Safety & Quality of Life**
A recurring theme across all three presentations was the reduction of the burden on city resources. By moving high-need populations into managed, supervised environments, the goal is to reduce the need for law enforcement to act as a primary response for mental health crises and to improve the general safety and aesthetic of public spaces.
