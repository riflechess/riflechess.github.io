#### Meeting Info
**Type:** Budget Committee Meeting  
**Purpose:** Review of the 2027 Proposed Operating Budget and Q2 Budget to Actuals  
**Date/Time:** August 19, 2026, at 10:00 AM  
**Context:** The committee is beginning the "budget season," reviewing various department updates, proposed tax levies, and spending adjustments for the upcoming fiscal year.

#### Summary
The city's proposed operating budget for 2027 is approximately **$888 million**, which includes a **6.8% property tax levy increase**. This hike is primarily designed to close a $26 million gap caused by rising costs in wages, health insurance, and inflation, as well as addressing debt obligations and the phase-out of federal funds (ARP). 

**Key Budget Highlights:**
*   **Public Safety:** The Fire department is shifting resources toward EMS capacity training and fleet maintenance. The Police budget includes $2 million for approximately 1.5 police academies; however, there is internal discussion regarding the efficacy of this spending given current recruitment struggles.
*   **Parks & Recreation:** To manage costs, some recreation centers will see reduced hours (notably at Edgecombe) or adjustments in programs. New funding was secured to make "RightTrack" and "Sprockets" programs sustainable now that one-time federal funds are expiring.
*   **Library Services:** The budget includes investments in safety specialists and automated visitor counters. However, it also includes the **proposed closure of the Dayton’s Bluff Library**, which is intended to save approximately $270,000.
*   **Infrastructure & Maintenance:** A new **Deferred Maintenance Fund** was established with a $1.1 million initial allocation to create a dedicated mechanism for addressing the city's facility backlog rather than handling repairs as one-off decisions.
*   **Property Tax Impact:** For a typical household (median home value of $290,000), the combined impact of property taxes and various utility/fee increases (water, sewer, trash) is estimated to be a **net increase of $174**.

**Points of Contention & Discussion:**
*   **Library Closures & Access:** Significant concern was expressed regarding the closure of the Dayton’s Bluff Library. Council members emphasized that such cuts impact areas of concentrated poverty and asked for a clear plan on how residents would maintain access to resources if the branch closes.
*   **Police Recruitment vs. Training Costs:** There was a debate over the $2 million allocated for police academies. Members questioned the "bang for your buck" since the city struggles to hire enough officers; there is a concern that the city may be spending significant funds to train individuals who ultimately do not stay with the department.
*   **Settlement Funding:** A point of contention was raised regarding the $750,000 set aside for legal settlements. It was noted that this amount has remained unchanged for years while actual costs continue to rise, leading to a need for more realistic budgeting in this area.
*   **Komo Zoo Finances:** The committee discussed the "negative fund" status of the Komo Zoo and how its deficit could potentially impact the city's bond rating if not addressed by the administration.
*   **CARES Team Status:** There was some pushback regarding the removal of the CARES team from the budget, with calls for a clearer long-term plan to ensure these services aren't simply disappearing but are being phased out properly.
